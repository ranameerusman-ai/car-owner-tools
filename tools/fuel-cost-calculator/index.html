Introduction

Fuel is one of the biggest parts of any trip budget, and this calculator works it out exactly for you. It works out what a trip will cost before you set off — the gallons you will need, the cost per mile, and what a year of driving at that rate adds up to.

Use it to budget a road trip, compare two cars before you buy, or see how much a lower fuel price or a slower cruising speed really saves you.

How to use this calculator

Three quick steps — the cost updates as you type.

STEP 1

Enter the distance

STEP 2

STEP 3

Enter the fuel price

Type in the price per gallon at the station you plan to use. The cost updates as you type.

How the cost is calculated

Three steps, all shown on the results panel.

Gallons needed = distance ÷ MPG. Trip cost = gallons × fuel price. Cost per mile = fuel price ÷ MPG. For example, 250 miles at 30 MPG needs 8.3 gallons; at $3.50 a gallon that is about $29.

How to save on every trip

The price at the pump is only part of it — how you drive matters just as much.

FREE TO FIX

Ease off a little

Driving at 65 mph instead of 75 mph can save around 10–15% on fuel on a motorway trip. It also adds only a few minutes to most journeys.

UP TO 3% OR MORE

Check tire pressure

Under-inflated tires use extra fuel on every mile. The correct pressure is on the driver’s door sticker — check it before a long drive.

EXTRA DRAG

Take the roof box off

A roof box or rack adds drag and can raise fuel use noticeably at motorway speeds. Remove it between trips.

PLAN AHEAD

Combine errands

One longer trip uses far less fuel than several short cold-start trips. A warm engine is a much more efficient engine.

SMALL SAVING

Compare fuel prices

Prices can vary by 20–30 cents a gallon between stations on the same road. Filling up before the motorway usually pays.

STEADY WINS

Use cruise control

On flat motorways, cruise control keeps a steady speed and keeps fuel use smooth and steady.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate fuel cost for a trip?

Divide the distance by your MPG to get the gallons needed, then multiply by the fuel price per gallon. For example, 250 miles at 30 MPG needs 8.3 gallons; at $3.50 a gallon that is about $29.

What is a good MPG for a road trip?

On a motorway run most petrol cars return 30–40 MPG, and hybrids 45 MPG or more. City driving returns a lower figure, so use your motorway figure for trip planning.

Does the calculator include tolls?

This calculator covers fuel only — a clean fuel figure to build your trip budget on. Add tolls, parking and food separately when you budget a trip.

Why is my real cost higher than the estimate?

Traffic, hills, air conditioning, extra weight and a roof box all add to fuel use. Treat the result as a planning figure and allow 5–10% extra for a long trip.

How do I work out my fuel cost for a whole year?

Multiply your cost per mile by the miles you drive in a year. This calculator shows a yearly figure based on 12,000 miles a year, the US average — change the miles to match your own driving.
