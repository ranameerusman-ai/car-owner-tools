What this means

—

Maths: MPG = miles driven ÷ gallons used. L/100km = 235.214 ÷ MPG. Cost per mile = fuel price ÷ MPG. As a rough guide, most modern petrol cars return roughly 25–35 MPG in mixed driving; hybrids and small diesels do more.

How to measure your MPG accurately

The dashboard figure is an estimate. This three-step method gives you your real number.

STEP 1

Fill the tank

Fill up completely and reset your trip meter to zero (or write down the odometer reading).

STEP 2

Drive normally

Drive as you usually do until the tank is at least half empty — a longer distance gives a more accurate result.

STEP 3

Fill up and calculate

Fill the tank again and note the gallons. Enter the miles and gallons above — that is your real MPG.

What affects your MPG?

Your MPG is not a fixed number — it changes with how and where you drive.

BIGGEST FACTOR

Driving style

Smooth, steady driving uses far less fuel than hard acceleration and braking. Motorway cruising at a steady speed is usually the most efficient.

UP TO 3% OR MORE

Tire pressure

Under-inflated tires increase rolling resistance and waste fuel. Check pressures monthly — the correct figure is on the driver’s door sticker.

EXTRA WEIGHT & DRAG

Load and roof racks

Extra weight and a roof box or rack increase fuel use. Remove roof racks when you are not using them.

CITY VS MOTORWAY

Trip type

Short city trips with lots of stops use much more fuel per mile than long motorway runs. Compare like with like.

REGULAR CARE

Maintenance

A dirty air filter, worn spark plugs or old engine oil can all reduce efficiency. Keep up with scheduled servicing.

COLD STARTS

Weather and warm-up

Engines use more fuel when cold, so short trips in winter return the lowest MPG of all.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good MPG?

It depends on the car. As a rough guide, most modern petrol cars return about 25–35 MPG in mixed driving, small cars and hybrids often 40–55 MPG, and large SUVs or trucks 15–25 MPG. Compare your result with your car’s official figures and with your own past results, not with other cars.

Why is my MPG lower than the official figure?

Official figures come from lab tests in ideal conditions. Real driving — short trips, cold starts, city traffic, air conditioning and extra weight — almost always returns lower MPG.

How do I convert MPG to L/100km?

Divide 235.214 by your MPG. For example, 30 MPG is about 7.8 L/100km. This calculator does it for you automatically.

Can I use this calculator for a diesel or hybrid?

Yes — the maths is the same for petrol, diesel and hybrid cars. Just enter the miles and the fuel used.
