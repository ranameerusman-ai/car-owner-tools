Introduction

MPG — miles per gallon — is the simplest way to know how much fuel your car really uses. It tells you how far you go on one gallon of fuel, and it is the number that decides what every trip costs you.

The MPG figure on your dashboard is only an estimate. This calculator works out your real MPG from your own fill-ups: the miles you drove and the gallons it took to fill the tank again. Use it to compare cars, follow your fuel economy over time, or work out your true cost per mile.

How MPG is calculated

The maths is one simple division.

MPG = miles driven ÷ gallons of fuel used. For example, 300 miles on 10 gallons is 30 MPG. To convert to litres per 100 km, divide 235.214 by your MPG — so 30 MPG is about 7.8 L/100km.

Tips for an accurate reading

Small habits make the result much more reliable.

AVERAGE IT OUT

Measure over 2–3 fill-ups

One fill-up can vary if a pump clicks early. Measure over two or three fill-ups and average the results for your most reliable figure.

SAME PUMP, SAME CLICK

Fill up the same way

Use the same pump when you can, and stop at the first click. A fuller tank can read as better MPG, so stopping at the first click keeps every reading consistent.

RESET EVERY TIME

Note the miles

Reset your trip meter at every fill-up, or write down the odometer reading. Exact miles give you an exact result.

How to measure your MPG accurately

The dashboard figure is an estimate. This three-step method gives you your real number.

STEP 1

Fill the tank

Fill up completely and reset your trip meter to zero (or write down the odometer reading).

STEP 2

Drive normally

Drive as you usually do until the tank is at least half empty — a longer distance gives a more accurate result.

STEP 3

Fill up and calculate

Fill the tank again and note the gallons. Enter the miles and gallons above — that is your real MPG.

What affects your MPG?

Your MPG changes with how and where you drive.

BIGGEST FACTOR

Driving style

Smooth, steady driving uses far less fuel than sharp acceleration and braking. Motorway cruising at a steady speed is usually the most efficient.

UP TO 3% OR MORE

Tire pressure

Under-inflated tires increase rolling resistance and use extra fuel. Check pressures monthly — the correct figure is on the driver’s door sticker.

EXTRA WEIGHT & DRAG

Load and roof racks

Extra weight and a roof box or rack increase fuel use. Take roof racks off between trips.

CITY VS MOTORWAY

Trip type

Short city trips with lots of stops use much more fuel per mile than long motorway runs. Compare like with like.

REGULAR CARE

Maintenance

A clean air filter, fresh spark plugs and fresh engine oil keep efficiency at its best. Keep up with scheduled servicing.

COLD STARTS

Weather and warm-up

Engines use more fuel when cold, so short trips in winter are where careful driving makes the biggest difference.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good MPG?

It depends on the car. As a rough guide, most modern petrol cars return about 25–35 MPG in mixed driving, small cars and hybrids often 40–55 MPG, and large SUVs or trucks 15–25 MPG. Compare your result with your car’s official figures and with your own past results — your car, your numbers.

Why is my MPG lower than the official figure?

Official figures come from lab tests in ideal conditions. Real driving — short trips, cold starts, city traffic, air conditioning and extra weight — almost always returns a lower figure than the lab test.

How do I convert MPG to L/100km?

Divide 235.214 by your MPG. For example, 30 MPG is about 7.8 L/100km. This calculator does it for you automatically.

Can I use this calculator for a diesel or hybrid?

Yes — the maths is the same for petrol, diesel and hybrid cars. Just enter the miles and the fuel used.

Should I trust the MPG figure on my dashboard?

Treat it as a rough guide. It is worked out from sensors and driving conditions, and it is usually a little optimistic. The fill-up method on this page — miles driven divided by gallons used — gives you your real number.
