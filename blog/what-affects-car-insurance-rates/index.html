What Affects Your Car Insurance Rates?

Insurance

20 November 2026 · 6 min read

Two drivers can pay wildly different prices for the same cover on the same car. Insurers price on risk — and they use a small set of factors to guess how likely you are to claim. Here are the seven that matter most.

1. Your driving record

The biggest single factor. A clean record is the cheapest discount there is: one accident adds roughly a third to a premium, and a DUI can almost double it. Points and claims usually affect your price for three to five years.

2. Your age

Drivers under 25 pay the most, because the claim statistics for young drivers are the worst. Rates usually fall sharply after 25, and keep falling with a clean record.

3. Where you live

Insurance in Michigan or Florida can cost roughly twice as much as in Vermont or Maine. Local repair costs, theft rates, weather and how often people claim all feed into the price for your state — and even your ZIP code.

4. Your car

Expensive cars cost more to repair or replace, so they cost more to insure. Cars that are stolen often, or that are expensive to fix, carry higher premiums. Older, cheaper cars are cheaper to cover.

5. Coverage level

Full coverage costs roughly three times liability-only cover, because it also pays for damage to your own car. Raising your deductible — the amount you pay before insurance kicks in — lowers the premium.

6. Annual miles

More miles means more time on the road and more risk. Low-mileage drivers often qualify for a discount, so it is worth reporting your real mileage honestly.

7. Your credit history

In most US states insurers may use a credit-based score when pricing. A better score usually means a lower premium — one more reason to pay bills on time.

Discounts worth asking about

Insurers rarely volunteer discounts, so ask. The most common are safe-driver discounts for several claim-free years, low-mileage discounts if you drive under about 7,500 miles a year, bundling discounts for holding home and auto cover with the same company, and discounts for paying the premium in one annual payment instead of monthly instalments. Some insurers also offer discounts for anti-theft devices, for completing a defensive driving course, and for students with good grades. Any one of these can be worth 5–15%, and they often stack.

How to compare quotes properly

Comparing quotes only works if they are for the same cover. Get quotes with the same liability limits, the same deductible and the same extras, or the cheapest-looking one may simply be the one that covers least. Write the three quotes side by side with their coverage limits, then compare the price. And re-shop every year or two: insurers change their prices, and the cheapest company for you last year may not be the cheapest this year. Loyalty is rarely rewarded in car insurance.

What can you actually change?

You cannot change your age or your state, but you can keep a clean record, compare quotes from at least three insurers, raise your deductible, and ask about safe-driver and low-mileage discounts. Quotes for the same driver can differ by hundreds of dollars a year, so shopping around is the single most effective step.
