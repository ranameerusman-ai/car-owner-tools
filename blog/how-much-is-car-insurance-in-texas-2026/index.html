How Much Is Car Insurance in Texas in 2026?

Insurance

20 November 2026 · 6 min read

Texas sits close to the US average for car insurance — cheaper than Florida or Michigan, more expensive than Vermont or Iowa. Here is what a typical Texas driver pays in 2026, and what pushes the price up or down.

Average cost in Texas

For a 40-year-old driver with a clean record, driving a 2021 car worth about $25,000, full coverage in Texas costs roughly $2,400 a year, or about $200 a month — using the same estimating method as our calculator. Liability only is roughly $820 a year. Your real quote will differ: it also depends on your ZIP code, exact car model and insurer.

Why Texas is not the cheapest state

Texas has a lot of cars, a lot of miles driven, and some of the country’s busiest highways. Hailstorms in the spring damage thousands of cars a year, and repair costs in big cities like Houston and Dallas are high. Rural Texas is usually cheaper than the big cities.

What raises the price most in Texas

  • Young drivers: a driver under 25 can pay 60% or more above the average.
  • A DUI or accident: a DUI can almost double a premium; one accident adds about a third.
  • Full coverage on a new car: a 2023 or newer car costs roughly 10% more to insure than a 2018–2022 one.
  • High mileage: driving over 15,000 miles a year adds about 10%.

How Texas drivers can pay less

Compare quotes from at least three insurers — Texas has a very competitive market, and prices for the same driver can differ by hundreds of dollars. Ask about safe-driver, low-mileage and bundling discounts, and consider a higher deductible if you can afford it. Keeping a clean record is still the biggest lever of all.

Texas minimum insurance requirements

Texas law requires every driver to carry liability cover of at least $30,000 for one person injured, $60,000 for all people injured in one accident, and $25,000 for property damage — written as 30/60/25. That is only the legal minimum, and it is often not enough: one serious accident can cost far more than $30,000, and anything above your limit comes out of your own pocket. Many drivers choose 100/300/100 limits instead, which cost more per month but protect far better. Full coverage is not required by law, but if you have a car loan or lease, your lender will almost always require it.

City vs rural: where you live in Texas matters

Insurance in Houston or Dallas is usually much more expensive than in a small town. Big cities mean more traffic, more accidents, more theft and higher repair costs, and insurers price that into every ZIP code. Hail is another Texas factor: spring hailstorms damage thousands of cars a year, and comprehensive claims for hail push up prices across the state. If you live in a hail-prone area, check that your comprehensive deductible is one you can afford — a $1,000 deductible on a hail claim is painful, but it keeps the monthly premium down.

One more Texas quirk: teen drivers. Adding a teenager to a family policy in Texas can nearly double the premium, because young drivers are the most expensive group to insure. Many Texas families save by choosing a higher deductible for the teen’s car, or by putting the teen on the oldest, cheapest car in the household instead of the newest one.
