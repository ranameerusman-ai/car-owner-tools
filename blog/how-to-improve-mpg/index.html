7 Simple Ways to Improve Your Car’s MPG

Fuel Economy

20 November 2026 · 6 min read

Fuel is one of the biggest costs of owning a car, and a few small habits can cut it by 10–20% without spending anything. Here are the seven changes that make the biggest difference, starting with the free ones.

1. Slow down a little

Fuel use rises sharply with speed. On a motorway, dropping from 75 mph to 65 mph can save around 10–15% in fuel — and it usually adds only a few minutes to the trip.

2. Drive smoothly

Hard acceleration and braking waste fuel. Look ahead, lift off early for junctions and traffic lights, and keep a steady speed. This alone is worth several MPG.

3. Check your tire pressure

Under-inflated tires increase rolling resistance and waste fuel. Check pressures monthly — the correct figure is on the driver’s door sticker. See our guide: How to Check Your Tire Pressure.

4. Take the roof rack off

A roof box or rack adds drag, especially at motorway speeds. Remove it when you are not using it — it can cost several MPG.

5. Empty the boot

Every extra 50 kg of weight costs fuel, mostly in town driving. Clear out the things you do not need for the journey.

6. Keep up with servicing

A dirty air filter, worn spark plugs or old engine oil can all reduce efficiency. Follow the service schedule in your manual.

7. Combine short trips

A cold engine uses far more fuel than a warm one. Several short trips from cold cost much more than one longer trip covering the same distance.

Plan your route

Stop-start city traffic is the worst thing for fuel economy, because every acceleration burns fuel and braking throws the energy away. If you can choose a slightly longer route with fewer traffic lights and a steady cruising speed, it often uses less fuel than the short, congested one. Motorways are the most efficient place to drive: a steady 55–65 mph in top gear uses far less fuel per mile than crawling through town.

Use air conditioning wisely

Air conditioning costs fuel — running it can add a few percent to consumption in town. At low speeds, opening a window is cheaper. At motorway speeds the extra drag of open windows costs about the same as the air conditioning, so use the air conditioning and keep the windows shut. Either way, it is a small effect compared with speed and driving style.

Does premium fuel help?

For most cars, no. Premium fuel has a higher octane rating, which only helps engines designed for it — usually high-performance cars whose manuals say premium is required. If your manual says regular, premium fuel will not improve your MPG or power, and you will simply pay more per gallon. Check the fuel filler cap: if it says regular unleaded, that is what to buy.

What about hybrids and electric cars?

Hybrids gain the most from smooth driving, because gentle braking recovers more energy and the engine switches off more often. Electric cars are measured in miles per kWh rather than MPG, but the same habits help: steady speed, correct tire pressure and no roof box all extend range.
